# Compatibility Scoring Methodology & Framework

**Project:** Resume Screening & Skill Gap Analysis System (TalentLens)  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Date:** October 2026  

---

## 1. Overview and Purpose

The TalentLens scoring system computes an objective, interpretable **Compatibility Score** between a candidate's resume and an employer's job description. The score is scaled to a continuous range of $[0.0, 100.0]$ and mapped to qualitative recommendation categories for recruiter decision support.

> **Important Disclaimer:** The compatibility score is an algorithmic decision-support estimate based on extracted textual evidence. It does not constitute a calibrated probability of hiring success, an evaluation of candidate character, or a guarantee of job performance. Automated decisions must not be made solely on this metric.

---

## 2. Mathematical Scoring Formula

The overall compatibility score $S_{\text{overall}}$ is calculated as a linear weighted combination of structured skill coverage, lexical baseline overlap, and deep semantic alignment:

$$S_{\text{overall}} = w_{\text{skill}} \cdot S_{\text{skill}} + w_{\text{baseline}} \cdot S_{\text{baseline}} + w_{\text{semantic}} \cdot S_{\text{semantic}}$$

Subject to:
$$\sum w_i = 1.0, \quad w_i \ge 0, \quad S_{\text{overall}} \in [0.0, 100.0]$$

---

## 3. Component Definitions and Normalization

### 3.1 Structured Skill Coverage ($S_{\text{skill}}$)
Measures the proportion of required technical skills mentioned in the job description that were evidenced in the candidate's resume:

$$S_{\text{skill}} = \begin{cases} 
\left( \frac{|\mathcal{K}_{\text{resume}} \cap \mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}|}{|\mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}|} \right) \times 100, & \text{if } |\mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}| > 0 \\
100.0, & \text{if } |\mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}| = 0 \text{ and } |\mathcal{K}_{\text{resume}}| > 0 \\
0.0, & \text{if } |\mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}| = 0 \text{ and } |\mathcal{K}_{\text{resume}}| = 0
\end{cases}$$

Where:
- $\mathcal{K}_{\text{resume}}$ is the set of canonical skills extracted from the resume (`src/skill_extraction`).
- $\mathcal{K}_{\text{jd}}$ is the set of canonical skills extracted from the job description.
- All terms are mapped through `ALIAS_MAP` and `SKILL_NORMALIZATION_MAP` to prevent mismatch between synonyms (e.g., `k8s` and `kubernetes`).

### 3.2 Lexical Baseline Similarity ($S_{\text{baseline}}$)
Measures bag-of-words keyword and n-gram overlap using TF-IDF (Term Frequency - Inverse Document Frequency) representations:

$$S_{\text{baseline}} = \cos(\mathbf{v}_{\text{resume}}, \mathbf{v}_{\text{jd}}) \times 100 = \left( \frac{\mathbf{v}_{\text{resume}} \cdot \mathbf{v}_{\text{jd}}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{\text{resume}}\| \|\mathbf{v}_{\text{jd}}\|} \right) \times 100$$

Where $\mathbf{v}$ represents the TF-IDF feature vector generated by the pre-trained `BaselineFeatureExtractor` (`models/baseline_feature_extractor.joblib`) fitted on engineering job corpora. $S_{\text{baseline}}$ is bounded in $[0.0, 100.0]$.

### 3.3 Semantic Similarity ($S_{\text{semantic}}$)
Measures contextual meaning, role congruence, and domain proximity using Sentence-BERT (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`):

$$S_{\text{semantic}} = \max\left(0.0, \cos(\mathbf{e}_{\text{resume}}, \mathbf{e}_{\text{jd}})\right) \times 100$$

Where:
- For multi-page or lengthy documents, text is partitioned into bounded word chunks with overlap (default: 200 words with 50-word overlap) to respect transformer attention limits.
- The document embedding $\mathbf{e}$ is computed via mean-pooling across chunk embeddings:
  $$\mathbf{e} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \text{Encode}(\text{chunk}_i)$$
- Negative cosine similarities are truncated to $0.0$.

---

## 4. Configurable Component Weights

The scoring weights are centrally managed in `src/scoring/config.py`:

| Mode | Skill Weight ($w_{\text{skill}}$) | Baseline TF-IDF ($w_{\text{baseline}}$) | Semantic S-BERT ($w_{\text{semantic}}$) | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hybrid (Default)** | **0.40 (40%)** | **0.20 (20%)** | **0.40 (40%)** | Balanced multi-modal assessment combining explicit keywords and contextual nuance. |
| **Classical Fallback** | **0.60 (60%)** | **0.40 (40%)** | **0.00 (0%)** | Automatically engaged when Sentence-BERT is unavailable or offline. |

---

## 5. Recommendation Thresholds

To provide intuitive decision support, continuous scores are mapped to four qualitative tiers:

| Score Interval | Category | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **80.0% – 100.0%** | **Strong Match** | Candidate satisfies the vast majority of required technical competencies and exhibits strong domain alignment. |
| **60.0% – 79.9%** | **Moderate Match** | Candidate demonstrates core requirements but may lack specialized secondary skills or require minor upskilling. |
| **40.0% – 59.9%** | **Potential Match** | Candidate possesses transferable or foundational skills but has notable gaps in key requirements. |
| **0.0% – 39.9%** | **Low Match** | Significant divergence in both required skill coverage and overall domain context. |

---

## 6. Known Limitations and Governance

1. **Unevidenced vs. Lacking:** The absence of a skill in a resume indicates that the term was not evidenced in the submitted text; it does not prove the candidate cannot perform the task.
2. **Scanned Documents:** The system extracts digital text. Image-only PDFs without OCR text layers will return an explicit error rather than an erroneous zero score.
3. **Novel Taxonomy Terms:** Emerging frameworks or custom proprietary tools outside the dictionary taxonomy will contribute via semantic and lexical similarity rather than explicit skill matching.
